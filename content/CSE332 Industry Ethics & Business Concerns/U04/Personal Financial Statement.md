# Personal Financial Statement

A personal financial statement is a document or spreadsheet outlining an individual's financial position at a given point in time.

## Benefits and Types
- Personal financial statements help track spending and increase  in net worth.
- Two primary types of financial statements:
  - **Personal Cash Flow Statement**: Tracks money *earned* and *spent*, *show*ing whether you have a positive or negative *cash flow*.
  - **Personal Balance Sheet**: Summarizes your *assets* and *liabilities* to *calculate* your *net worth*.

## Key Points:
- Assets and liabilities outside business.
- Personal net worth for each owner.
- Key for business financing clarity.
- Owners may use personal assets.
- Shows available assets for business.
- Investors and bankers require details.

# Startup Expenses and Capitalization

- **Startup Expenses**:
	- Startup costs are the initial investments and expenditures a new business makes before it can begin operating.  
	- Examples: Office space rental, marketing, employee salaries.

-  **Capitalization**:
	- Total required investment to launch the business.
	- Represents the full cost of resources planned to be leveraged.

### Entrepreneurs Should Clearly List:
- **Startup Expenses**: Every cost associated with getting the business operational.
- **Associated Costs**: Each item’s specific cost breakdown.
- **Unusual or Expensive Items**: Explanation of capital-intensive items or large upfront costs.

# Financial Plan

Financial planning is the task of determining how a business will afford to achieve its strategic goals and objectives.

## Key Components:
- **12 Month Profit & Loss Statement**: Project profitability, determine path to success.
- **Three-Year Profit Projection (Optional)**: Long-term growth planning.
- **Projected Cash Flow**: Plan for cash needs, preliminary expenses, and reserves.
- **For Each Item**: Track expected cash receipt timing and expense payment schedules.
- **Opening Day Balance Sheet**: Fundamental financial report for launch readiness.
- **Break-Even Analysis**: Determine sales level required to cover costs.

# Appendices

The appendices **contain** *letters, charts*, and *other* important *data* *supporting* the business *plan*.

## Include:
- Brochures and advertising materials
- Industry studies
- Blueprints and plans
- Maps and photos of location
- Magazine or other articles
- Equipment lists (owned or to be purchased)
- Leases and contracts
- Letters of support from future customers
- Market research studies
- List of assets available as loan collateral

# Refining the Plan

The generic business plan should be customized for your business type and target audience.

## For Raising Capital – For Bankers:
- **Amount of Loan**: Clearly state the loan amount.
- **How Funds Will Be Used**: Explain fund allocation.
- **What This Will Accomplish**: Describe business impact.
- **Requested Repayment Terms**: Provide schedule.
- **Collateral Offered**: List assets offered as security.
- **Existing Liens**: Mention any current liens on assets.

## For Investors:
- **Funds Needed Short-Term**: Immediate capital needs.
- **Funds Needed in 2–5 Years**: For scaling and expansion.
- **How Funds Will Be Used**: Product, marketing, operations.
- **Estimated ROI**: Project investor returns.
- **Exit Strategy**: IPO, acquisition, or buyback.
	- IPO: *Initial Public Offering*
		- An IPO is when a *private company* *offers* its *shares* *to* the *public* for the *first time on a stock exchange*. It marks the transition from a private company to a public company.
	- Buyback: 
		- A buyback is when a company *purchases* its *own shares* *from* the *stock market*. This reduces the number of shares available to the public.
- **Investor Involvement**: Equity, advisory roles.

## For Type of Business

### Manufacturing:
- Planned production levels
- Cost comparison with industry
- Product line pricing
- Gross profit margins
- Production capacity constraints
- Inventory and new product plans

### Service Businesses:
- Key competitive factors
- Pricing methods
- Production management
- Quality control procedures
- Labor productivity tracking
- Subcontracting, credit, and client strategy

# Startup Story – Flipkart

## History:
Launched in 2007, Flipkart began during the early days of Indian e-commerce. Though registered in Singapore, the company is headquartered in Bangalore, India.

## Founders:
- **Sachin Bansal** and **Binny Bansal**, both ex-Amazon employees and alumni of IIT Delhi, originally from Chandigarh, India.

## How it Started:
- Initially focused on selling books.
- Gradually expanded to a wide range of products.

## Funding History:
- Early funding reached ₹200 million.
- Valuation eventually grew to $15 billion.

## Evolution:
- Introduced **Cash on Delivery (COD)** and card payment on delivery, revolutionizing Indian e-commerce.
- Built an in-house delivery system for reliable service.

## Acquisitions:
- Acquired **Myntra**, **LetsBuy**, and others.
- Evolved into India’s largest e-commerce platform.

# Startup Story – MakeMyTrip

## Founder:
- **Deep Kalra**, founder of MakeMyTrip.com, based in Gurgaon.

## Journey:
- Started his career after earning a master’s degree from IIM Ahmedabad.
- Worked at **GE Capital** and **ABN AMRO Bank**.
- Left to join **AMF Bowling** to set up entertainment centers in India.

## Realization:
- Despite opening over 200 bowling lanes, he felt unfulfilled and underutilized under foreign management.

## Turning Point:
- Saw the internet’s potential and quit his job.
- Founded **MakeMyTrip** to simplify online travel bookings.
- Quickly became a leader in the Indian travel industry.

## Achievements:
- Chairperson of NASSCOM Internet Working Group
- Member of NASSCOM Executive Council
- Member of CII’s Tourism sub-committee
- **Awards:**
  - Best Travel Portal India – World Travel Awards (2013, 2014)
  - E-tailer of the Year – ET Retail Awards (2013, 2014)
  - Best Online Travel Service – Times Travel Honours (2011)
  - One of the Ten Best Companies to Work For in India (2010–2013)
