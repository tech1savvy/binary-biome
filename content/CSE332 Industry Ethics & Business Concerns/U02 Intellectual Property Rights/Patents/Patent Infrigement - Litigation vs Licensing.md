# Litigation vs Licensing

- **Litigation** is preferred if the goal is to stop a competitor from selling an infringing product by *legal actions through courts*.
- **Licensing** is suitable when seeking royalties for the use of an invention.

## Alternatives to Litigation

- Litigation is expensive, time-consuming, and disruptive.
- **Alternative dispute resolutions (ADR)** like mediation and arbitration offer cost-effective solutions.
    - **Mediation**: A neutral *third party helps* both parties negotiate a *mutually acceptable agreement*. It is *non-binding* and *focuses on settlement*.
        - > Mediator helps parties make decision.
    - **Arbitration**: A neutral *arbitrator* listens to both sides and *makes* a *decision*, which *can be binding or non-binding* depending on prior agreement. It is faster and less formal than litigation.
        - > Arbitrator makes decision for the parties. 
- Think of **mediation** like a *teacher* helping two kids agree on how to share a toy. The teacher doesn’t force a decision—both kids must agree.
- **Arbitration** is like a *referee* in a game who listens to both sides and then makes a final call. Sometimes, the decision must be followed (binding), and sometimes, the players can choose to accept it or not (non-binding).
