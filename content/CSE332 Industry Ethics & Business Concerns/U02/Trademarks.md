# Types of Trademarks

## Product Mark

- Identifies *products or goods* instead of services.
- The product is the company's unique selling feature.
- Uses TM (™) initially to indicate an unregistered mark.
- Once registered, the *® symbol* is used.
- **Examples:** Pepsi®, Maggi®, PHILIPS®.
	- ![[Pasted image 20250319235527.png|250]]

## Service Mark

- Identifies and distinguishes services rather than products.
- Uses *SM (℠)* instead of TM.
- Features:
    - Service Identification – Helps consumers recognize the service.
    - Source Indicator – Shows the service provider's identity.
    - *Non-Tangible* Association – Linked to intangible services.
- **Examples**: FedEx, McDonald's Golden Arches, Netflix, American Airlines Logo.

## Collective Mark

- *Owned by* a *group* or collective.
- Identifies goods or services offered by members of the collective.
- Distinguishes members' goods/services from those of non-members.
- Also used to indicate membership in an organization.
- **Examples**: 
	- CA (Chartered Accountant) 
		- ![[Pasted image 20250320001050.png|100]]
	- CPA (Certified Public Accountant)

## Certification Mark

- *Defines a standard* for goods and services.
- Assures consumers that products meet specific quality, origin, or safety standards.
- **Examples**:
    - UL (Underwriters Laboratories)
    - Fair Trade
    -  *Energy Star*
    - Organic Certification

## Shape Mark

- Protects the three-dimensional shape or configuration of a product or its packaging.
- Granted when the shape itself is distinctive and serves as a source identifier.
- Considered a non-traditional trademark as it extends beyond text or logos.

- **Example**: Shape of Coco-Cola Glass Bottle

## Pattern Mark

- Protects a specific pattern or design used to identify goods or services.
- The *consistent* use of the *pattern* allows it to function as a trademark.
- **Examples**:
    - Gucci GG Pattern

    - Louis Vuitton's Monogram Pattern
    - *Marshall*'s Basket-weave Pattern
        - ![|300](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcRDMMEf8OGo0DySVby9cPh_RA-h08Bn5jtvQg&s)

## Sound Mark

- Protects a specific sound or combination of sounds.
- Used to identify and distinguish the goods/services of a brand.
- Registered only when the *sound* is *distinctive* enough to be recognized by consumers.

- **Examples**:
	- Nokia Welcome Jingle

# Loss of Rights

- **Improper use** – Incorrect usage may lead to loss of distinctiveness.
- **Failure to police** – Not taking action against infringement weakens rights.
- > **Genericide** – When a *trademark becomes* a *common term* (e.g., Xerox, Aspirin).
- **Failure to renew** – Non-renewal leads to expiration of rights.
- **Non-use** – Prolonged non-use may result in cancellation.
- **Cancellation** – Authorities can cancel a trademark if it does not meet requirements.
- **Improper assignment or licensing** – Unauthorized transfers may lead to loss of ownership.

# Benefits of Registration of a Trademark

- **Legal Ownership** – Registration is prima facie evidence of proprietorship.
- **Exclusive Rights** – Allows exclusive use in connection with goods/services.
- **Legal Protection** – Enables legal action against infringement.
- **Transferability** – The proprietor can assign or license the trademark like any other property.
- **Goodwill & Perpetual Ownership** – As long as the trademark is renewed periodically, the owner can continue to enjoy its goodwill indefinitely.
